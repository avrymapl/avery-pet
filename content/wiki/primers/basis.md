A basis of a vector space is a set of vectors that is just enough to build
everything: every vector in the space can be written as a sum of scaled basis
vectors, and in exactly one way. The plane's usual basis is the unit arrow
east and the unit arrow north — every arrow is so many easts plus so many
norths, uniquely.

The uniqueness is what makes a basis a coordinate system: the scaling factors
in the one possible recipe for v are the coordinates of v, and knowing them
is knowing v. A space can have many bases, and much of the craft is choosing
the one in which a given problem's coordinates are easiest to find or
easiest to read.
