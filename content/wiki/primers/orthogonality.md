Two vectors are orthogonal when their inner product is zero — the algebraic
residue of "perpendicular", kept because it travels to spaces where the
geometric picture cannot. Orthogonal directions do not overlap: no amount of
one contributes anything along the other.

What the word buys in practice is independence of computation. To find how
much of a vector lies along one member of a mutually orthogonal family, take
a single inner product with that member; the answer cannot be contaminated
by the other members, precisely because their contributions read as zero.
Whenever coefficients are computed one at a time, each by its own formula,
orthogonality is the licence being invoked.
