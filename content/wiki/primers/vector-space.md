A vector space is any collection of objects that supports the two operations
arrows in the plane support: adding two of them, and scaling one by a number.
The axioms — addition commutes, scaling distributes, there is a zero, and so
on — are exactly the rules those arrows obey; anything satisfying them earns
the name vector, whatever it looks like.

The leap to make is that "vector" is a role, not a shape. Lists of numbers
qualify, but so do functions: two functions add by adding their outputs at
each input, and a function scales by scaling its outputs. Once a collection
passes the axioms, every tool phrased in terms of adding and scaling — bases,
coordinates, geometry — applies to it wholesale, and that transfer is the
entire trick being played here.
