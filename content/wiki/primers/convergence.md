An infinite sum cannot be added up term by term — there is no last term — so
its value is defined through partial sums: add the first term, the first two,
the first three, and so on, and watch the sequence of running totals. If the
running totals approach a single finite value, the series converges to that
value; if they grow without bound or oscillate forever, it diverges and has
no value at all.

Two habits matter when reading about convergence. First, always ask "in what
sense": a sequence of functions can converge at every individual point, or in
an averaged sense over a whole interval, and the two can genuinely disagree.
Second, convergence is a claim about the tail — the first million terms are
irrelevant; only the eventual behaviour counts.
