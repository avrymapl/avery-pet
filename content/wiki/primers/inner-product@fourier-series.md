An inner product is a rule pairing two vectors into a number that measures
their alignment, on the model of the dot product: multiply matching
coordinates, add the results. Here the vectors are periodic functions, and
the inner product of $f$ and $g$ is $\int f(x)\,g(x)\,dx$ over one period —
the continuous analogue of "multiply matching coordinates and add", with the
function's value at each point playing the role of a coordinate and the
integral playing the role of the sum.

Read it as a similarity score: where $f$ and $g$ swing together the product is
positive, where they swing oppositely it is negative, and the integral tots
up the agreement over a full period. This one definition is what lets the
geometric language of the article — lengths, projections, perpendicularity —
apply to functions at all.
