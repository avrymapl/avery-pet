The derivative of a function measures how fast its output changes as the
input changes — the slope of the graph at a point. Over a stretch from x to
x + h the average rate of change is the difference quotient
(f(x + h) − f(x)) / h; the derivative f′(x) is the limit of this quotient as
h shrinks to zero, the instantaneous rate that the averages settle toward.

Two facts about differentiation are worth having at hand. It is linear: the
derivative of a sum is the sum of the derivatives, and constants pass
through. And for the sine and cosine it acts by rotation: sin becomes cos,
and cos becomes −sin, so differentiating a wave leaves the same wave shifted,
merely rescaled by its frequency.
