An inner product is a rule that takes two vectors and returns a single
number measuring their alignment — positive when they point broadly the same
way, negative when opposed, zero when neither. The model is the dot product
in the plane: multiply matching coordinates and add. From the inner product
of a vector with itself comes length (squared), so one rule underwrites the
whole metric vocabulary — lengths, distances, angles.

Any rule may serve, provided it is symmetric in its two arguments, linear in
each, and gives every nonzero vector a positive inner product with itself.
The freedom matters: spaces whose vectors are not arrows can still be given
an inner product, and with it inherit geometry.
