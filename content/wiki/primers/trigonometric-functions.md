Sine and cosine are the coordinates of uniform circular motion: as a point
travels counterclockwise around the unit circle, $\cos x$ is its horizontal
coordinate and $\sin x$ its vertical one, with $x$ the distance travelled
along the rim. Both functions repeat every $2\pi$ (a full lap), oscillate
smoothly between $-1$ and $1$, and are identical in shape — cosine is sine
shifted a quarter lap.

The family extends by frequency: $\sin nx$ and $\cos nx$ complete $n$ full
oscillations in the space where $\sin x$ and $\cos x$ complete one. Higher
$n$ means faster wiggling, and the whole collection lives on the same
interval of length $2\pi$. When these pages speak of "frequencies", they
mean the members of this family, indexed by $n$.
